Activity & Exercise

This view shows how your activity metrics move together over time, so you can see whether your overall activity is trending up or down.

These metrics use very different units, so each line is shown relative to your first month (set to 100). A line at 120 means that metric is 20% higher than when your data began; below 100 means lower. Lines rising together mean your overall activity is trending up.

Heart & Recovery

Each line is shown relative to your first month (=100) so metrics on different scales sit together. Watch the direction: a falling resting heart rate and a rising HRV or VO₂max both usually mean improving fitness and recovery — the two moving that way together is the encouraging pattern.

Sleep

These views focus on sleep quality and regularity: how consistent your schedule is, how weekdays compare with weekends, and how efficient your nights are.

Average hours asleep on weeknights vs weekend nights. On weekends you go to bed about 119 min earlier and sleep 40 min less. A large weekend catch-up (“social jet lag”) usually points to not getting enough sleep during the week.

Sleep efficiency = time asleep ÷ time in bed, averaged per month. Higher is better: it means less time lying awake. Below ~85% often means trouble falling or staying asleep.

Bedtime consistency: your bedtime varies by about 2.3 h night to night — quite variable; a steadier schedule usually improves sleep quality.

Body & Vitals

Body metrics use different units, so each line is shown relative to your first month (=100). This makes it easy to spot which measurements are drifting up or down — and which move together — regardless of their scale. A line that stays near 100 has barely changed.
